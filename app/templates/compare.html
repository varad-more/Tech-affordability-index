      What an equivalent salary is not

      • There is no single right answer, and this page shows two. Holding your needs share constant assumes your discretionary spending scales with the destination, which overstates the raise for someone whose savings do not care what rent costs. Holding your surplus constant assumes none of it does, which understates it for anyone who eats where they live. The honest answer is between them, and both are printed above rather than one being chosen for you.
      • Necessities are a subsistence budget. Everything except rent is MIT's county-level figure for a single adult with no children: no savings, no travel, no meals out. Two counties can share a needs figure and feel nothing alike.
      • Moving costs nothing here. No relocation expense, no lease break, no double rent, no state-tax part-year filing, no change in job market. This is the steady state after the move, not the move itself.
      • County is not neighbourhood. Both figures are county medians. The spread inside a large county is often wider than the gap between the two counties you are comparing.
      • Local income tax is modelled for two cities only, New York City and Philadelphia. If either end of your move levies one that is not modelled, a warning appears above and the real gap is wider than the page says.
