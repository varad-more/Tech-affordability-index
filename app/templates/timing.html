When is a lease
cheapest to sign?

Rent is not flat across the year. Summer is moving season, so demand and asking rents peak; winter is when landlords have empty units and less leverage. This measures how large that swing actually is where you are looking, and whether it is big enough to be worth planning around.

    Where timing matters most

    Counties with the largest swing between their cheapest and dearest month. A big swing usually means a market dominated by one moving season: a university town, or somewhere with a short summer.

    How to read this, and how not to

    • The swing is real but modest. Across the counties with a usable pattern the median peak-to-trough swing is about 2%. On a $2,000 rent that is roughly $40 a month. It is worth knowing; it is not worth taking a worse job for.
    • Where you move dwarfs when. The gap between the cheapest and dearest counties on this site is a factor of several. The gap between the cheapest and dearest month within one county is a couple of percent. Get the place right first.
    • These are historical averages, not a forecast. The index says what January has typically done relative to its year, over the past decade. It does not know about next year's supply, rates, or a new employer arriving.
    • ZORI is smoothed, which damps seasonality. The real swing in listing prices is probably somewhat larger than what is shown here. That makes these figures conservative rather than overstated.
    • Counties with too little history are excluded rather than shown with a shaky pattern. A calendar month needs at least three observations before it enters the average.
    • Asking rent, not your renewal. This tracks what is being listed. It says nothing about what a landlord will do to an existing tenant's rent.
